RoadAnnoter: An AI-Assisted Desktop Tool for Semantic Segmentation Annotation of Road-Scene Images

Nazmul Haque, Arnob Protim Roy
2026-11-17cs.CV
摘要
Accurate semantic segmentation of road-scene images is essential for road safety research; however, polygon-level annotation remains labor-intensive and limits labelled dataset development. This study presents RoadAnnoter v1.0, an open-source desktop application integrating six human-in-the-loop annotation modes: manual polygon delineation, SAM Prompt, Smart Select, Edge Fill, pretrained semantic segmentation using Cityscapes-trained SegFormer models, and task-specific segmentation using TinySegNet, a lightweight MobileNetV3-Small-based network trained with accumulated annotator-generated labels. SAM-based modes use promptable segmentation with SAM and MobileSAM, while Edge Fill combines Canny edge detection with colour-similarity-based flood filling in CIELAB colour space. SegFormer generates suggestions for common road-scene categories, whereas TinySegNet supports adaptation to project-specific features through iterative training. All six modes use a shared mask-to-polygon pipeline comprising morphological processing, Gaussian smoothing, contour extraction, and Douglas-Peucker simplification; annotations are exported in YOLO segmentation format. RoadAnnoter was used to annotate Google Street View images from recorded road accident locations in Bangladesh for a vision-language model-based investigation of free-flow speed estimation. Qualitative assessment across representative scenes demonstrated the complementary applicability of the annotation modes to diverse feature geometries. Visual comparison with Roboflow indicated similar qualitative boundary characteristics for principal road-scene regions, although no quantitative accuracy assessment was conducted. RoadAnnoter provides a locally operated framework for developing road-scene segmentation datasets for transportation engineering and road safety research.

CoopDistill: Equilibrium-Guided Multi-Agent Policy Distillation for Cooperative Driving

Shiyu Fang, Sheng Zhao, Jiarui Zhang, Chengkai Xu 等
2026-11-16cs.ROcs.MA
摘要
Cooperative driving extends autonomous driving from individual vehicle decisions to joint maneuvers that improve traffic safety and efficiency. Optimization-based methods impose safety constraints but often fix paths to simplify multi-vehicle planning, limiting spatial flexibility. Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) explores alternatives through interaction, but coordinated learning and safe execution remain challenging. Their integration nevertheless faces challenges in exploration efficiency, experience allocation, and execution reliability. To address these challenges, this paper proposes CoopDistill, an equilibrium-guided multi-agent policy distillation framework. A potential-game (PG) formulation provides a fixed-path longitudinal reference, around which a residual MARL policy learns longitudinal acceleration, conflict-approach timing, and lateral path corrections. A hard-case curriculum concentrates experience on interactions that PG leaves unresolved or substantially delayed. A comparative rollout selector evaluates the joint consequences of learned corrections against PG before choosing the executed action. Experiments in merging, roundabout, and intersection scenarios compare CoopDistill with six baselines at four connected automated vehicle penetration levels. Relative to PG, success improves by 2.1--27.1 percentage points and delay decreases by 7.3--27.3\%, with no observed collisions. Code is available at https://github.com/FanGShiYuu/CoopDistill

A Reliability Study of Explainable AI for Freight Mode Classification

Ali Alawi Balfaqih, Mansur M. Arief
2026-11-11cs.AIcs.LG
摘要
Machine learning models are increasingly used to support freight transportation decisions, but their predictions are rarely accompanied by evidence that the associated explanations can be trusted. In this study we investigate explainable machine learning for classifying shipments as Air/Air Charter or Truck/Ocean using the PEPFAR Supply Chain Management System Delivery History dataset, with 5,964 shipment records and 47 input features after cleaning, where freight cost and pack price are excluded as near-proxies of the transportation mode. We compare three Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) architectures, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and gradient boosting with imbalance-aware metrics and paired significance tests across one hundred random replications. We then evaluate the explanations themselves, namely the stability of SHAP feature rankings across retraining and across models, the faithfulness of SHAP and LIME attributions under a feature-deletion test, and the geographic structure of the destination effects, which we map for all 38 destination countries. The results suggest that the tree ensembles significantly outperform the neural and linear models (86.8% against 83.3-85.0% mean testing accuracy) while their leading features remain the most stable across retraining. Removing the five features ranked most important by SHAP or LIME changes the predicted probabilities roughly four times as much as removing randomly chosen features. The destination effects form a coherent geographic pattern that simple port access does not explain. However, models with similar accuracy disagree substantially on which features matter most, and thus explanation stability and faithfulness should be assessed before such explanations are used to support freight transport decisions.
