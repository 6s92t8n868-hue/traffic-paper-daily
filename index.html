Cross-Scenario Controller Parameter Transformation from Real-World Driving Data through Flow Matching

Christian Zink, Maik Pfefferkorn, Eric Lenz, Felix Kallmeyer 等
2026-10-23eess.SYcs.LG
摘要
Advanced Driver Assistance Systems have become increasingly widespread, aiming to support drivers, reduce workload, and improve traffic safety. However, many systems are rarely used because their behavior feels unnatural and does not match individual driving behavior. The growing availability of large-scale, real-world human driving data enables more human-like and driver-individual assistance functions. This paper formulates the adaptation of Adaptive Cruise Control parameters as a driver-individual scenario-to-scenario transport problem, transforming controller parameters from one driving context to another. Using flow matching, we train a generative model to predict the target-scenario behavior of previously unseen drivers from only a few demonstrations in a source scenario. We compare the approach with a linear parameter-shift baseline and evaluate flow-matching variants incorporating driver-consistent coupling, driver embeddings, and classifier-free guidance based on driving profiles extracted from trajectory data using a variational autoencoder.

StateBridge: Phase- and Track-Grounded Reasoning for Synthetic-to-Real Traffic Video Captioning and VQA

Blessing Agyei Kyem, Richard Dzinyela, Joshua Kofi Asamoah, Eugene Denteh 等
2026-10-22cs.CV
摘要
Traffic safety videos provide rich evidence for understanding interactions between pedestrians and vehicles, but synthetic-to-real evaluation makes this setting challenging because models must learn from simulated scenes and generalize to real-world traffic. In this work, we present StateBridge, a traffic video-language framework designed to operate under this synthetic-only training protocol. Our approach uses phase-aware clip construction, pedestrian and vehicle track summaries, and structured prompts to ground vision-language models in stable traffic evidence rather than appearance alone. We fine-tune public vision-language models on synthetic captioning and visual question answering examples, then combine their outputs through fact-locked caption fusion and evidence-diverse VQA consensus to improve consistency across actor roles, behavioral phases, and question types. StateBridge achieves an official S2 score of 55.4679 on AI City Challenge 2026 Track 2 using only synthetic training data. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of structured evidence transfer for traffic safety captioning and visual question answering.

Joint Dynamic Lane Reversal and Variable Speed Limit Control: A Graphormer-Based Approach

Hamed Araab, Abbas Ahmadi, Mohammad Hossein Basiri
2026-10-26eess.SYcs.AIcs.LG
摘要
Connected and automated vehicles could allow reassigning lane directions and speed limits on every road frequently, but the benefit hinges on how traffic reroutes, and one-way roads can isolate junctions. We formulate network-wide dynamic lane allocation and directional speed limit control with one-way roads, lane clearing before each reversal, and a navigable graph kept strongly connected at all times. An action-resolution algorithm grounded in graph orientation theory admits a proposal closing a direction only if the network stays strongly connected and otherwise relaxes or reverts it, keeping all junctions mutually reachable within the model, even during clearing. All road controllers share one transformer whose attention, Global Endpoint Relation-Aware Spatio-Temporal Self-Attention (GERASTSA), spans all road-time tokens, biased by the endpoint relations between roads. It is pretrained on action-conditioned traffic forecasting and fine-tuned with per-road proximal policy optimization under a mixed reward that also credits neighboring roads and the network. On a 6 x 6 grid with commuter rush hours, the learned policy makes about two thirds of roads one-way in the peak direction and cuts daily delay by 90% against the initial configuration held fixed and by 85% against a tuned two-way load balancer. Restricted to two-way roads at test time, it gains only about as much as this balancer, whereas its learned speed limits add delay. Pretraining roughly halves the updates needed to surpass the fixed configuration. Of the ablations, only removing the relational bias or the spatial embedding clearly matters, multiplying delay about fivefold.
