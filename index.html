Driver Takeovers and Longitudinal Responses Analysis from Naturalistic Driving

Yuhang Wang, Hao Zhou
2026-11-05cs.HC
摘要
As Level 2 advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS) become more widely used, understanding driver-system interaction is increasingly important for traffic safety. However, limited real-world data make these interactions difficult to study. We present ADAS-TO, a naturalistic dataset of 16,446 takeover clips from 364 drivers across 2,585 different drives. It combines road video, vehicle kinematics, driver inputs, and ADAS status. To analyze takeover behavior, we retained 6,206 manually reviewed takeover events during continued lane keeping or car following. We excluded events in which the driver intentionally changed lanes, turned, or made a stop beyond the ADAS's capabilities (e.g., at a red light or stop sign) shortly after takeover. Drivers began intervening before ADAS disengaged in 65.6% of takeovers involving braking or steering. When the ego vehicle was still closing on a lead vehicle at disengagement, longitudinal safety margins often narrowed further after the transfer to manual control. Among 2,031 such events, 78.5% had a lower minimum time-to-collision (TTC) in the five seconds after disengagement than in the five seconds before it. For events involving braking, the mean TTC trajectory reached its minimum about 1.2 s after disengagement. We model the subsequent braking response using a Gazis-Herman-Rothery (GHR) car-following law. Its median five-second spacing root-mean-square error is 1.34 m with observed lead motion and 1.84 m with predicted lead motion. The fitted model expresses braking as a response to looming, with stronger responses at higher speeds and shorter gaps. Applied before takeover, it shows that greater looming than predicted by the human-response reference is associated with disengagement. These findings support continued assessment of longitudinal safety after ADAS disengagement, while the driver's braking response develops.

Multi-Modal Multi-Agent System for Bridge Operation and Maintenance based on Large Language Model

Xiangyun Luo, Yunkai Zhang, Yong Huang, Stephen Wu 等
2026-11-02cs.MA
摘要
Bridges are critical transportation infrastructure, and reliable operation and maintenance (O&M) is essential for traffic safety and long-term structural performance. However, bridge O&M faces key bottlenecks: no unified architecture coordinates heterogeneous resources; model inferences lack clear links to physical-mechanical laws; and experts' tacit knowledge remains difficult to formalize. This study proposes a knowledge-enhanced autonomy formalization methodology and apply it to construct the Bridge O&M Multi-Modal Multi-Agent System (BOMMA), which integrates heterogeneous diagnostic actions, physics-mechanics-informed representations, long-term memory, and structured experiential knowledge within a coordinated multi-agent workflow, enabling interpretable and traceable scheduling, analysis, and decision-making. On 3,492 multi-source instructions, the proposed method achieves scheduling recalls of 85.37% for simple and 99.40% for composite tasks, with 95.35% BERT F1-score for O&M action reasoning. Ablation studies confirm the contributions of the components in the autonomy. This study bridges general LLM reasoning and specialized bridge O&M requirements.

SIMBAD: Spatio-Temporal Traffic Forecasting Robust to Aperiodicity

Daniel Y. Lee, Seungwon Shin, Seunghoon Han, Sungsu Lim 等
2026-11-06cs.AI
摘要
Accurate traffic prediction is essential for urban planning but remains challenging due to the irregular patterns caused by unexpected events. While Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Networks (STGNNs) effectively model periodic data, they often struggle with these aperiodic fluctuations. To address this, we propose SIMBAD, which improves generalization irregular patterns via two key mechanisms: 1) adaptive regulation of periodic signals to prioritize recent signals, and 2) dynamic spatial modeling that adjusts influence between connected nodes based on contextual relations. Built upon simple similarity metrics to adapt to aperiodic patterns, SIMBAD outperforms existing methods on real-world benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior performance in predicting unseen and irregular traffic events.

Understanding Data Influence in Transportation Networks: A Physics-Constrained Influence-Function Framework

Hamidreza Attari, Yueyue Fan, Johannes Royset
2026-11-05cs.LG
摘要
Understanding how individual training observations influence model predictions is important for developing reliable and interpretable data-driven transportation systems. Existing influence-function methods, however, are primarily formulated for unconstrained learning and do not explicitly account for physical constraints that couple different components of a transportation network. Under such constraints, perturbing a training observation can affect model parameters and predictions only through physically feasible directions, potentially changing how its influence propagates across the network. This paper develops a hard physics-informed influence-function framework for constrained learning, in which training-data influence is characterized within the physically feasible space defined by network constraints. The analytical results reveal how physical constraints alter the magnitude, direction, and spatial distribution of training-sample influence relative to conventional influence measures. Using traffic prediction under flow-conservation constraints as a case study, we show that influential observations identified by the proposed framework differ systematically from those identified by classical influence functions. Through orthogonal decomposition, we quantitatively characterize how much of a perturbation in the training data is propagated through the network via the flow-conservation constraints and how much is removed by the constraint projection. The proposed influence framework provides a physically consistent approach for evaluating training-data influence in constrained transportation learning problems, with potential applications to data diagnostics, sensor evaluation, and targeted data collection.

CoReFlow: Consistency-Relaxed Flow Maps for Probabilistic Traffic Forecasting

Pengnan Chi, Zhiguo Zhang, Xiaoliang Ma
2026-11-03cs.LG
摘要
Probabilistic traffic forecasting is essential for reliable Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS), as it captures uncertainties in complex road networks. Recent diffusion- and flow-based generative models have facilitated uncertainty modeling through a multi-step sampling process. However, beyond slow inference, this process is designed to capture complex multimodal distributions, which misaligns with the limited complexity of the conditional traffic data distribution, causing models to underperform deterministic baselines. To address this, we introduce the \underline{Co}nsistency-\underline{Re}laxed \underline{Flow} Map (CoReFlow) model, presenting the first effort to leverage the one-step generation capability of flow maps, known as solution operators of probability-flow ordinary differential equations. Besides, we improve accuracy using a concise training objective that supervises the model via interpolation endpoints over finite time intervals. Unlike existing methods (e.g., self-distillation and MeanFlow Identity) that maintain trajectory consistency to model multimodal distributions, our objective leverages conditional mean to restrict the consistency, thereby achieving higher accuracy in the probabilistic traffic forecasting task. Extensive experiments on four real-world datasets show CoReFlow achieves state-of-the-art forecasting accuracy. It surpasses recent baselines, provides competitive uncertainty modeling, and reduces inference costs compared to existing generative baselines.
