Evacuation Traffic Prediction for Dynamic Transportation Networks over Multiple Hurricanes: A Generalized Graph Learning Approach

Md Nafees Fuad Rafi, Samiul Hasan
2026-11-25cs.LGcs.AI
摘要
High traffic demand during hurricane evacuations causes severe congestion on major highways, leading to delays and safety risks for evacuees. Predicting evacuation traffic in real-time can significantly enhance evacuation management efforts. However, evacuation traffic is highly dynamic and uncertain due to evolving hurricane characteristics and diverse population response. Each hurricane has unique characteristics and evacuation contexts, resulting in different spatial-temporal traffic patterns. Moreover, the structure of detector-based transportation networks varies across multiple hurricanes. Hence, a model trained on the data from one hurricane may not generalize well for others. This study proposes a generalized dynamic graph learning framework using traffic detectors and evacuation data from 12 historical hurricanes. Leveraging the dynamic nature of transportation network, we develop a Graph Convolutional Long Short-Term Memory (GCN-LSTM) model that predicts evacuation traffic up to 6 hours in advance. For an unseen hurricane (Milton, 2024), the model shows strong generalizability, achieving an RMSE of 280.6 and R2 of 0.96 for 1-hour prediction, and an RMSE of 459.8 and R2 of 0.89 for overall 6-hours prediction. We also introduce a modular training approach, where we evaluate performance improvements by sequentially incorporating data from multiple hurricanes. The proposed model outperforms state-of-the-art baseline models for overall 6-hour prediction horizons. Ablation experiments show an 8.4% improvement in RMSE for the proposed dynamic GCN-LSTM model compared to its static counterpart. Traffic management officials can use the developed model to understand future traffic conditions during hurricane evacuation in advance and take proactive measures to reduce delays during evacuation.

Decentralized Clustered Federated Learning for Cellular Traffic Forecasting over LEO Constellations

Jonas Kirch, Tiago Koketsu Rodrigues, Yuichi Kawamoto
2026-11-25cs.LG
摘要
Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite constellations are positioned to act as orbital edge-compute nodes in emerging 6G non-terrestrial networks, supporting forecasting and control tasks for ground cellular infrastructure. However, training such models is challenged by heterogeneous ground-cell traffic patterns, intermittent links to ground stations, and the satellites' own orbital motion, which continuously shifts the data each satellite observes. In this work, we extend Decentralized Federated Clustering (DFCA) to the LEO setting and apply it to cellular internet-traffic forecasting on the Telecom Italia Milan+Trentino dataset. Satellites cooperate exclusively through $k$-nearest inter-satellite gossip with no ground-side aggregator, while zero-shot head selection lets each satellite specialize to the latent traffic regime of the ground cell currently beneath it. Averaged over $K{=}20$ post-training orbital snapshots, our extension reduces forecasting Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) by $15.3\%$ over a decentralized FedAvg baseline of equivalent capacity, with cluster recovery accuracy of $85.3\%$. The results indicate that clustered specialization yields measurable gains under the structural heterogeneity inherent to realistic cellular-over-LEO scenarios.

Transferable, Time-Parallel Graph Dynamics via Space-Time Factorization

Wencong Yang, Chaopeng Shen, Abdolmehdi Behroozi
2026-11-23cs.LGphysics.geo-ph
摘要
Time-parallel neural operators (FNO and successors) have transformed PDE surrogates on regular grids. Mesh-based operators (MeshGraphNets, Transolver) extend operator learning to irregular geometries, but jointly learning across diverse topologies while retaining non-recurrent temporal evaluation remains challenging. We propose space-time factorization: partition the model into local temporal operators (learned, graph-independent) and a spatial mixer that propagates information across nodes without graph-dependent learned parameters. This structural condition lets the same parameters run on unseen graphs; non-recurrent instances also admit time-parallel evaluation. Accurate zero-shot transfer further depends on controlling three information leaks (graph-dependent weights, graph-specific features, and graph-correlated training distributions) and on suitable task physics and training coverage. The protocol applies to two distinct spatial-mixer families, fixed-spectral and learned-attention. We validate the principle in three domains. For floodwave propagation in river networks, a 27K-parameter model trained on the Yangtze transfers zero-shot to the Mississippi with median NSE 0.998 in boundary-conditioned operational evaluation, training 5.5x faster than a stabilized recurrent baseline. On cross-geometry Navier-Stokes, the three-seed SepFNO mean relative vorticity error on five unseen targets falls by about 28% from 5 to 12 training geometries, with a consistent single-run SepTransolver trend. On cross-city traffic forecasting, zero-shot transfer reaches within 12.9% of an oracle trained on the target city. Across settings, cross-geometry error decreases with both the number of training geometries and their relatedness to the test, at a rate depending on the spatial mixer. This opens the path to pooled-data, foundation-style models for graph dynamics on irregular topologies.

STMemoLLM: A Spatial-Temporal Memory-Augmented LLM for Robust Traffic Forecasting

Jian Zhou, Binqing Wu, Zongjiang Shang, Wanghui Qiu 等
2026-11-23cs.LG
摘要
Traffic forecasting is essential for intelligent transportation systems. However, traditional deep-learning models often rely heavily on high-quality observations. Recent LLM-based approaches incorporate pretrained knowledge to improve forecasting, but they remain sensitive to input perturbations and struggle to preserve long-term patterns. To address this, we propose STMemoLLM, a spatial-temporal memory-augmented LLM for robust traffic forecasting. STMemoLLM introduces memory prototypes to compress inputs into compact summary tokens while extracting reliable dynamics from residual signals, achieving a balance between stability and variability. Specifically, a memory-enhanced tokenizer encodes prototypes and sensor characteristics into attention biases to guide adaptive compression of correlated sensors into semantic summary tokens. A memory-calibrated router further evaluates contextual deviation, compression deficiency, and prototype alignment to identify reliable residual signals, which complement the summaries while suppressing noise. Extensive experiments on eight real-world datasets show that STMemoLLM achieves state-of-the-art performance. Additional experiments examine point masking, additive Gaussian noise, structured missingness, and limited training data, demonstrating strong robustness. Code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/STMemoLLM-70BF.
