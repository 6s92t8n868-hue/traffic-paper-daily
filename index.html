RIECNN: Real-Time Image Enhanced CNN for Traffic Sign Recognition

Reem Abdel-Salam, Rana Mostafa, Ahmed H. Abdel-Gawad
2026-10-10cs.CVcs.AIcs.LG
摘要
Traffic sign recognition plays a crucial role in the development of autonomous cars to reduce the accident rate and promote road safety. It has been a necessity to address traffic signs that are affected significantly by the environment as well as poor real-time performance for deep-learning state-of-the-art algorithms. In this paper, we introduce Real-Time Image Enhanced CNN ({\our}) for Traffic Sign Recognition. {\our} is a real-time, novel approach that tackles multiple, diverse traffic sign datasets, and out-performs the state-of-the-art architectures in terms of recognition rate and execution time. Experiments are conducted using the German Traffic Sign Benchmark (GTSRB), the Belgium Traffic Sign Classification (BTSC), and the Croatian Traffic Sign (rMASTIF) benchmark. Experimental results show that our approach has achieved the highest recognition rate for all Benchmarks, achieving a recognition accuracy of 99.75\% for GTSRB, 99.25\% for BTSC and 99.55\% for rMASTIF. In terms of latency and meeting the real-time constraint, the pre-processing time and inference time together do not exceed 1.3 ms per image. Not only have our proposed approach achieved remarkably high accuracy with real-time performance, but it also demonstrated robustness against traffic sign recognition challenges such as brightness and contrast variations in the environment.

Neuro-Memory Fuzzy Inference System for Mimicking Human-like Car Following Behavior

Nazmul Haque, Md Asif Raihan. Md. Hadiuzzaman
2026-10-08cs.LGcs.AI
摘要
This study presents the Neuro-Memory Fuzzy Inference System (NeMeFIS), a hierarchical machine learning architecture that asymmetrically models acceleration and deceleration in car following behavior by integrating five human memory types procedural, working, episodic, semantic, and declarative. By linking external variables to memory functions via metaheuristics and validating them through factor and p-value analyses, NeMeFIS uncovers latent cognitive influences across Arterial, Collector, and Rural Highway corridors for different types of vehicles. Results from 54 different trained models emphasize cognitive thresholds shaped by driver perception limits and cognitive load. The trained NeMeFIS models outperform traditional statistical and conventional machine learning models in replicating realistic driving behavior, including comparisons with Linear Regression, ANFIS, and LSTM architectures. Fuzzy rule analysis reveals that declarative memory demands the highest rule, especially during deceleration, indicating complex braking decisions. Procedural memory drives acceleration, while semantic and declarative memory guide deceleration. Risk perception also emerges as a key factor, particularly on urban roads. Validated on both heterogeneous and homogeneous datasets, NeMeFIS offers a robust framework for modeling driver cognition. The findings support psychotherapeutic applications and the development of adaptive, human-like decision systems in Connected and Autonomous Vehicles (CAVs) to enhance traffic safety.

Traffic State Prediction and Control Using a Koopman Operator Framework with Polynomial Observables

Shakib Mustavee, Arvind Singh, Shaurya Agarwal
2026-10-14eess.SY
摘要
In this paper, we develop a Koopman operator framework for modeling and control of traffic flow governed by the Lighthill--Whitham--Richards (LWR) conservation law. We show that the nonlinear dynamics induced by the Greenshields flow--density relation naturally motivate a dictionary of polynomial observables, while the controlled dynamics become bilinear in the lifted Koopman coordinates. A reduced-order Koopman model is identified from spatio-temporal traffic-density snapshots and used as the prediction model in a model predictive control (MPC) framework for ramp metering. The resulting Koopman-MPC is compared with two predictive control baselines that use, respectively, a fixed linearized LWR model and the nonlinear finite-volume LWR model. In a one-hour freeway experiment, the Koopman-based controller maintains the traffic density below the critical Greenshields value and closely reproduces the behavior of the nonlinear PDE-based MPC, with a maximum-density difference below 1%. The Koopman predictor also reduces the mean online model-evaluation cost by approximately one order of magnitude relative to the nonlinear PDE predictor. In contrast, the linearized LWR predictor is computationally cheaper but exhibits substantially larger prediction errors and yields a more conservative metering policy. These results demonstrate that the analytical structure of the nonlinear traffic conservation law can be exploited to construct an interpretable data-driven surrogate for efficient traffic prediction and control

Coarse-Grained Predictive Resource Allocation Framework enabling In-Premise XR Collaborations

Sourav Mondal, Elaine Wong
2026-10-11cs.NI
摘要
Immersive extended reality (XR) applications are key enablers of human-centric connectivity in next-generation networks. However, meeting their high-throughput, ultra-low-latency, and high-reliability demands while preventing cyber-sickness remains a critical challenge, especially in environments with non-deterministic wireless connectivity. Fiber-To-The-Room (FTTR), extending widely deployed Fiber-To-The-Home (FTTH) infrastructure, offers a promising solution. Yet, satisfying the stringent Quality-of-Experience (QoE) requirements of XR collaborators demands robust resource allocation across the multi-tier cascade of FTTH, FTTR, and WiFi segments. In this paper, we propose a coarse-grained traffic forecasting-empowered proactive resource allocation and intelligent in-premise roaming framework. Here, artificial intelligence (AI) model complexities are scaled to the computational capabilities of each network segment. At the FTTR segment, we employ a hybrid temporal convolutional and bi-directional long short-term memory (TCN-BiLSTM) network for multi-modal XR traffic forecasting, integrating passive optical network (PON) and WiFi access points with multi-link operation (MLO) and orthogonal frequency-division multiple access (OFDMA). The resulting long-horizon forecasts reduce inference frequency, enabling coordinated FTTR-WiFi resource allocation and seamless network-orchestrated mobility across rooms. At the FTTH segment, we deploy Transformer-based time-series models (e.g., Informer, Autoformer, PatchTST, and TFT) to predict the complex XR traffic aggregated in FTTR segments for transmission over the FTTH backhaul. Evaluations show Transformer-based models are up to $\sim$75% more accurate in long-horizon forecasting than the hybrid model. Extensive simulations confirm the proposed framework significantly outperforms traditional methods in satisfying XR QoE requirements.

The Evolution of In-Premise Networks In the Era of Immersive Services: From Copper to Fiber-to-The Room [Invited Tutorial]

Elaine Wong, Sourav Mondal
2026-10-11cs.NI
摘要
The telecommunications industry is shifting toward an experience-driven era defined by immersive applications that impose stringent network demands. While passive optical network (PON)-based Fiber-To-The-Home (FTTH) architectures have historically delivered high-capacity broadband, a growing performance gap between access and indoor networks necessitates driving fiber deeper into premises. Complementing existing copper and WiFi technologies, Fiber-to-the-Room (FTTR) is emerging as a critical enabler. This article examines the drivers of this architectural shift, specifically focusing on collaborative extended reality (XR). We analyze the multi-modal traffic characteristics and Quality-of-Experience (QoE) requirements of XR, alongside the resulting network performance implications. To address these demands, we propose a novel resource allocation framework integrating coarse-grained traffic forecasting with intelligent in-premise roaming. This framework facilitates the flexible deployment of artificial intelligence models tailored to the computational capabilities of the FTTH, FTTR, and WiFi segments. Numerical evaluations of a representative multi-tier network demonstrate that long-horizon, multi-step-ahead traffic forecasts significantly reduce inference frequency. This approach enables coordinated resource allocation and network-orchestrated seamless mobility across rooms, effectively satisfying collaborative XR QoE requirements. Finally, we highlight strategies to amortize FTTR capital expenditure by extending the optical infrastructure's role beyond basic connectivity. By supporting diverse services over a unified physical layer, FTTR enhances infrastructure utilization, unlocks novel revenue streams, and maximizes the value of in-premise fiber deployments.

Learning to Sync at the Edge: Client-Side Adaptive Synchronization for Federated Learning

Ebasa Temesgen, Ammar Ahmed, Nathan Lechamo, Ali Anwar 等
2026-10-09cs.DCcs.LGcs.NI
摘要
The scalability and performance of edge federated learning are fundamentally constrained by synchronization bottlenecks. Clients operate under heterogeneous compute, time-varying communication conditions, and, in some settings, privacy constraints, yet synchronization is often still handled with fixed schedules. However, synchronization decisions are often made outside the client, even though the client directly observes local progress, resource state, and communication conditions. We present a client-side reinforcement learning synchronization framework in which each client decides during training whether to upload, download, synchronize bidirectionally, or defer communication. The framework places a lightweight scheduler alongside the local model and formulates synchronization as a resource-aware control problem driven by local learning and system state. We study this framework on two representative edge workloads, spatiotemporal traffic forecasting and image classification. Across both settings, adaptive client-side synchronization improves the accuracy-cost trade-off, reducing system cost in traffic forecasting while maintaining competitive cost and improving final accuracy in image classification. We also consider privacy-constrained settings, where synchronization must account for not only latency and energy but also privacy expenditure. Overall, our results show that synchronization in edge federated learning is better treated as an adaptive client-side control problem than only as a server-side coordination mechanism.
