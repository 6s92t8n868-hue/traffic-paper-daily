AgenticConflictLens: From Long Traffic Videos to Verifiable Conflict Evidence

Boshra Khalili, Sonia Moshfeghi, Jinwoo Jang, Andrew W Smyth
2026-11-10cs.CV
摘要
Traffic cameras continuously record urban road-user behavior, providing proactive safety evidence beyond crash reports. Yet safety-critical events - conflicts, near misses, unsafe turns, sudden braking, and crosswalk interactions - are rare and brief, buried in hours of routine footage. Existing traffic-video pipelines extract detections, trajectories, and risk scores but process footage uniformly, without adapting to an analyst's query. Vision-language methods improve scene understanding but often lack traffic-specific verification of participant-level conflicts. We introduce AgenticConflictLens, an agentic, query-conditioned framework for efficient, auditable conflict extraction from long fixed-camera traffic videos. Given a natural-language safety query, a Traffic-Aware Controller combines structured traffic memory with specialized conflict-analysis tools to choose where to search, which road users to examine, and which evidence to verify or reject. A coarse-to-fine search retrieves candidate intervals and applies detailed verification selectively. The framework produces conflict records with refined time intervals, participant pairs, semantic regions, trajectories, motion evidence, uncertainty, rejection reasons, and supporting clips. On 96 annotated hours from Bellevue and NYC DOT, AgenticConflictLens achieves the highest conflict-existence F1 and temporal IoU on both datasets: 0.84 F1/0.77 tIoU on Bellevue and 0.80 F1/0.73 tIoU on NYC DOT, while inspecting only 8.7% and 11.9% of video, respectively. These results support scalable, evidence-grounded urban traffic-safety analysis.

NightSteps: A Night-Time Dataset and Benchmark for Pedestrian Crossing Intention Prediction

Sjoerd Takken, Shaodi You
2026-11-08cs.CV
摘要
Autonomous vehicles use pedestrian crossing intention prediction (PCIP) to participate safely in traffic. Existing models are largely trained under favourable daytime conditions with sufficient daylight. Little is known about the robustness of these models under night-time and low-light conditions, due to the lack of datasets containing night-time footage. This paper investigates how a dataset, named NightSteps, can be constructed and evaluated as a baseline benchmark. The dataset is based on nighttime dashcam footage collected from six different cities around the world, capturing a wide variety of road conditions. The dataset consists of dashcam-recorded point-of-view footage, raw data of pedestrians' locations with corresponding 'track ids' and ground-truth labels containing accurate information about pedestrian crossing behaviour, resulting in a diverse dataset. Gemini, ChatGPT, and human assessment are used to demonstrate the usability of the dataset by predicting crossings in clips from the dataset and comparing the results with the initial ground-truth labels. These results show that human input achieves an accuracy of 88.17% and an F1-score of 80.21%. Predictions from Gemini and ChatGPT perform lower, with accuracies of 60% and 79.13% respectively, and F1-scores of 42.50% and 63.64%. The evaluation demonstrates that the NightSteps dataset can be used for future model evaluation to test the performance of PCIP, with the aim of improving road safety at night.

A Reliability Study of Explainable AI for Freight Mode Classification

Ali Alawi Balfaqih, Mansur M. Arief
2026-11-11cs.AIcs.LG
摘要
Machine learning models are increasingly used to support freight transportation decisions, but their predictions are rarely accompanied by evidence that the associated explanations can be trusted. In this study we investigate explainable machine learning for classifying shipments as Air/Air Charter or Truck/Ocean using the PEPFAR Supply Chain Management System Delivery History dataset, with 5,964 shipment records and 47 input features after cleaning, where freight cost and pack price are excluded as near-proxies of the transportation mode. We compare three Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) architectures, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and gradient boosting with imbalance-aware metrics and paired significance tests across one hundred random replications. We then evaluate the explanations themselves, namely the stability of SHAP feature rankings across retraining and across models, the faithfulness of SHAP and LIME attributions under a feature-deletion test, and the geographic structure of the destination effects, which we map for all 38 destination countries. The results suggest that the tree ensembles significantly outperform the neural and linear models (86.8% against 83.3-85.0% mean testing accuracy) while their leading features remain the most stable across retraining. Removing the five features ranked most important by SHAP or LIME changes the predicted probabilities roughly four times as much as removing randomly chosen features. The destination effects form a coherent geographic pattern that simple port access does not explain. However, models with similar accuracy disagree substantially on which features matter most, and thus explanation stability and faithfulness should be assessed before such explanations are used to support freight transport decisions.
