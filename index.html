Edge Deployable Lightweight Framework for Real-Time Driver Safety System with Integrated Lane Departure and Traffic Sign Recognition

Hassaan Ali Qureshi, Danish Hussain, Ali Hassan, Aon Safdar 等
2026-10-18cs.CV
摘要
Lane departure and traffic-sign negligence are leading contributors to road accidents, particularly in regions with non-standardized signage and limited annotated data. Existing Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS) solutions are often developed as disjointed submodules, are rarely validated for real-time operation, and struggle to balance accuracy with low-latency inference for embedded deployment. We address these gaps with a unified, edge-deployable ADAS framework that simultaneously performs lane-departure warning (LDW) and traffic-sign detection and recognition (TSD/TSR), and validate the proposed system on automotive-class devices. Our framework combines a lightweight, lane-segmentation-driven LDW, which includes a drivable-area fallback for degraded markings, with a two-stage TSD/TSR pipeline consisting of efficient sign localization followed by fine-grained classification, designed to preserve accuracy at embedded latency. To support region-specific deployment under data scarcity, we curate a 35-class local traffic-sign dataset and introduce a generalized augmentation and balancing profile, expanding the corpus from 359 images to 8,750 samples. On an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, the integrated system sustains 15 FPS end to end (32 FPS for LDW and 18 FPS for TSD/TSR). The TSD/TSR branch achieves 97.4 mAP for detection and 95.6% validation accuracy (93.1% on a local real-world set) while maintaining an approximately 13.9 M parameter footprint. Relative to recent methods that target a single task or report results only on desktop-class GPUs, our approach delivers both LDW and TSD/TSR with real-time performance on edge hardware, providing a practical pathway to low-cost, field-ready ADAS in resource-constrained settings.

Automatic Cause Determination in Road Scene Understanding Using Qualitative Reasoning and Four-Valued Logic

Nassim Belmecheri, Arnaud Gotlieb, Nadjib Lazaar, Helge Spieker
2026-10-14cs.CV
摘要
Road scene understanding in automated driving (AD) aims to build a comprehensive analysis of video sequences taken on the road by embedded or fixed cameras (e.g., mounted on vertical road signals). One goal is to identify the relevant actors in the scene and another goal is to determine the causes that have triggered a specific action of the ego car (i.e., stop, slow down, turn left, etc.). In a complex urban environment, these causes can be multiple, confusing, possibly contradictory to other causes and not easily expressible using simplistic reasoning. Still, providing accurate {\it automatic cause determination} supports a) user acceptance by providing appropriate explanations to the car passengers and road users; b) increased road safety by providing detailed road scene understanding to traffic. In this paper, we propose using spatiotemporal reasoning and Belnap's four-valued logic to formulate complex causes of AD action in a road scene. We compute these causes by analysing a Qualitative eXplainable Graph (QXG), which is an abstract representation of the road scene capturing spatiotemporal relations between road entities. Starting from a QXG, our approach called \tool, is targeted to determine complex causes of a selected AD action occurring in a specific frame of a road scene. The usefulness of \tool is demonstrated on several scenes extracted from the well-known NuScene dataset.

Spectral Adaptive Graph Transformer for Efficient and Scalable Traffic Forecasting

Amirhossein Ghaffari, Susanna Pirttikangas, Ekaterina Gilman
2026-10-18cs.AIcs.LG
摘要
Traffic forecasting models based on spectral graph convolution offer an interpretable account of how information is mixed between sensors, but they rely on an eigendecomposition of the graph Laplacian. It costs cubic time, is fixed by the road network, and provides a symmetric operator that cannot represent the directed propagation of traffic congestion. Attention-based spatio-temporal transformers remove these limitations but generate a dense sensor-by-sensor attention map at each layer and time step, so memory, rather than accuracy, becomes the binding constraint on large networks. We introduce the spectral adaptive graph transformer, whose spatial operator is learned rather than derived from the Laplacian, directed rather than symmetric, and conditioned on the current traffic state rather than a static state, at a cost linear in the number of sensors. A sparse road graph diffusion branch provides the strictly local propagation that a low-rank operator cannot express, and a zero-initialized gate learns the balance between the two. Before spatial mixing, a unit-mean-gain frequency-bin attention module reweights each sensor's short-term periodicities according to its identity and current context. Across six public benchmarks spanning 170 to 1843 sensors, evaluated over three random seeds, the proposed model attains the lowest error of any single end-to-end model on one benchmark and remains competitive with the strongest current attention models on the others, while using about a quarter of their memory and training about twice as fast.

Traffic State Prediction and Control Using a Koopman Operator Framework with Polynomial Observables

Shakib Mustavee, Arvind Singh, Shaurya Agarwal
2026-10-14eess.SY
摘要
In this paper, we develop a Koopman operator framework for modeling and control of traffic flow governed by the Lighthill--Whitham--Richards (LWR) conservation law. We show that the nonlinear dynamics induced by the Greenshields flow--density relation naturally motivate a dictionary of polynomial observables, while the controlled dynamics become bilinear in the lifted Koopman coordinates. A reduced-order Koopman model is identified from spatio-temporal traffic-density snapshots and used as the prediction model in a model predictive control (MPC) framework for ramp metering. The resulting Koopman-MPC is compared with two predictive control baselines that use, respectively, a fixed linearized LWR model and the nonlinear finite-volume LWR model. In a one-hour freeway experiment, the Koopman-based controller maintains the traffic density below the critical Greenshields value and closely reproduces the behavior of the nonlinear PDE-based MPC, with a maximum-density difference below 1%. The Koopman predictor also reduces the mean online model-evaluation cost by approximately one order of magnitude relative to the nonlinear PDE predictor. In contrast, the linearized LWR predictor is computationally cheaper but exhibits substantially larger prediction errors and yields a more conservative metering policy. These results demonstrate that the analytical structure of the nonlinear traffic conservation law can be exploited to construct an interpretable data-driven surrogate for efficient traffic prediction and control
